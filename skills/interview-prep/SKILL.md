# Interview Prep

## Purpose

Applications get the user into rooms. This skill covers what happens next: walking in with a plan, answers built from a maintained bank of their real stories, and a debrief habit that makes every interview improve the next one. It never invents stories, numbers, or outcomes. Where the record has a gap, the bank says so and the user fills it.

## Core files

- `references/story-bank.md`: the master stories, in STAR+R format (Situation, Task, Action, Result, plus Reflection: what it proves), tagged by behavioral theme. Every practice answer and prep plan draws from here.
- The role's application folder (the posting text and the submitted resume) is the source for what the role asks and what the user already claimed on paper. Interview answers must match the submitted materials exactly; an interviewer can hold both.

## Workflow

### 1. Interview invite: build the prep plan

Trigger: an interview invite for a tracked role, from the inbox watch or forwarded by the user.

1. Pull the record: tracker row (company, role, submitted date, pay band), the application folder, and the reframing playbook entry for the role family (what this family screens for is what the interview will probe).
2. Research the round: who is interviewing, if named (recruiter screen, hiring manager, panel, executive), format, length. Use what the invite and the company's public materials state; label anything inferred as inferred.
3. Build the question map: the likely questions for this role family and round, each mapped to the story-bank story that answers it. Questions with no story are the real prep work; flag them at the top of the plan.
4. Run the red-flag check (section 4) and paste the verdict into the plan.
5. Save the plan and give the user the short version: when, who, the three stories to lead with, any story gaps, the red-flag verdict, and logistics status.

### 2. Practice sessions

Trigger: the user asks to practice, or a prep plan exists and the interview is within 48 hours.

1. One question at a time. Ask, wait for the full answer, then score. Never batch questions and never supply the answer before the user attempts it.
2. Score each answer on four things: specificity (names, numbers, dates, versus generalities), structure (situation and task in under 30 seconds, action in the middle, result lands the point), length (behavioral answers target 90 seconds to 2 minutes spoken), and evidence (the story reached for matches the question's theme, the story bank, and the submitted resume).
3. Feedback format: what landed, what drifted, the one fix that matters most. Then offer a redo of the same question. One redo is normal; two means the story itself needs work, so note it for the bank.
4. If an answer surfaces facts not in the bank (a number, an outcome, a better story), capture them verbatim during the session and update the story bank at the end, with the user's confirmation of any figure before it goes in.
5. End with a one-line summary: questions covered, weakest answer, what to drill next time.

### 3. Post-interview debrief

Trigger: the interview happened; same day when possible, memory decays fast.

1. Walk the debrief with the user: questions asked (exact wording where remembered), how each answer landed, interviewer signals, anything learned about the role, team, or pay that was not in the posting.
2. Save it with the role's prep materials.
3. Feed the bank: new stories drafted into STAR+R from what the user actually said, refined stories edited, stories that landed badly noted with what to change. The user's confirmation is required before new facts or figures enter the bank.
4. Re-run the red-flag check with the new information. Process behavior in the room is evidence; update the verdict if it moved.
5. Remind the user about the thank-you note the same day: short, addressed to each interviewer where contact details exist, one specific reference to something discussed. Surface it; the user sends it. Never send it for them.
6. Note any follow-up the interviewer promised (timeline, next round, materials) so the inbox watch can catch a stall. When a promised date passes with nothing from the employer, surface the stall to the user with a short draft nudge: polite, one or two lines, referencing the promised timeline and nothing else. No new claims, no pressure, no mention of other processes unless one is real. The user approves the text before anything sends. One nudge per stall; after that, the process goes back to waiting and only a genuinely new development reopens it.

### 4. Company red-flag check

Run it when a prep plan is built, again after each debrief, and any time the user asks. Work from evidence only:

- Posting history: reposts, how long the role has been open, whether the same seat keeps being re-hired.
- Process so far: speed, ghosting, moving goalposts, last-minute format changes.
- Pay transparency: the posted band versus what they say in the room.
- Churn signals: tenure patterns on the team, layoff news, repeated hiring for the same seat.

Every flagged item needs its source or the observation it came from. Close with a verdict in one of three forms: Proceed, Proceed with eyes open, or Serious concern, each with the evidence lines under it. A verdict summarizes evidence; it is not a recommendation to withdraw. That call is the user's.

## Output contract

- Prep plan: a saved file plus the short chat version (when, who, lead stories, story gaps, red-flag verdict, logistics status).
- Practice session: per-answer scores with one fix each, plus the closing one-liner.
- Debrief: a saved file, the story-bank updates made, the updated red-flag verdict, the thank-you reminder.
- Red-flag check: the verdict with evidence lines, nothing more.

## Operating rules

- Never invent a story, a number, or an outcome. The bank is built only from the user's documented record and from facts they state in practice or debrief, confirmed before they are written down. Metrics the record lacks stay marked as to-be-quantified.
- Interview answers must match the submitted resume and application answers. If a bank story and the resume disagree, the resume wins and the bank gets corrected.
- Practice means the user's answers, sharpened. Feedback can restructure, cut, and reorder; it does not replace their experience with better-sounding fiction.
- Red flags are signals with sources, not verdicts about a company's character. Present the evidence plainly, including when the check comes back clean.
- One interview at a time in practice; one question at a time inside a session.
- Keep chat reports short: the plan, the gaps, the verdict. The files hold the detail.
